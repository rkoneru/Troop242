🏥 First Aid Handbook

Tap any card to see detailed procedures and when to seek help

🩹
Basic Wound Care
Clean wounds and apply proper bandages
Steps: 1. Wash hands 2. Clean wound with clean water 3. Apply direct pressure to stop bleeding 4. Use sterile gauze or bandage 5. For deep cuts, seek medical attention 6. Watch for infection signs
🔥
Burn Treatment
Cool and cover burns properly
Steps: 1. Cool burn with running water 10-20 minutes 2. Remove jewelry/tight items 3. Cover with clean, non-stick material 4. Do NOT use ice directly 5. Pain relief if safe 6. Seek help for severe burns
🦴
Fracture & Sprains
Immobilize and treat injuries
Steps: 1. Stop activity immediately 2. Immobilize with splint or sling 3. Apply ice wrapped in cloth 4. Elevate if possible 5. Rest area 6. Seek medical evaluation
💊
CPR Basics
Emergency cardiopulmonary resuscitation
Steps: 1. Call 911 immediately 2. Position victim on back 3. Clear airway 4. Chest compressions 100-120 beats/min 5. If trained, 2 breaths after 30 compressions 6. Continue until help arrives
🚑
Shock Response
Treat for shock and trauma
Steps: 1. Call 911 2. Keep victim warm with blanket 3. Lay down flat 4. Elevate legs 12 inches (if no spine injury) 5. Do NOT move unnecessarily 6. Monitor breathing and responsiveness
🐝
Insect Stings & Bites
Handle bee, wasp, and spider incidents
Steps: 1. Remove stinger carefully with flat object 2. Wash area with soap 3. Apply ice 4. Elevate if on limb 5. Use antihistamine for itching 6. Watch for allergic reaction signs
🤕
Head & Neck Injury
Handle head trauma carefully
Steps: 1. Call 911 for any significant head trauma 2. Do NOT move neck/head 3. Stabilize head with hands 4. Monitor consciousness 5. Watch for nausea/vomiting 6. Do NOT remove helmet
🌡️
Hypothermia & Heat Exhaustion
Temperature-related emergencies
Hypothermia: 1. Move to shelter 2. Remove wet clothing 3. Warm gradually 4. Warm beverages if conscious Heat: 1. Move to cool place 2. Apply cool water 3. Elevate legs 4. Drink water
⚠️
Choking
Clear airway obstruction
Steps: 1. Ask "Are you choking?" 2. Stand behind victim 3. Place fist above navel 4. Grasp fist with other hand 5. Thrusts inward and upward 6. Repeat until object dislodged
🩸
Severe Bleeding
Control heavy bleeding
Steps: 1. Wear gloves if available 2. Apply direct pressure with clean cloth 3. Do NOT remove cloth 4. Add more cloth if needed 5. Apply pressure bandage 6. Elevate limb 7. Call 911

🔥 Survival Skills

Tap any card to see detailed techniques and best practices

🏕️
Shelter Building
Create windproof, waterproof shelter
Steps: 1. Choose dry, level ground 2. Avoid dead trees 3. Use natural materials or tarp 4. Create windbreak 5. Ensure good drainage 6. Insulate from ground
💧
Water Safety
Purify and collect drinking water
Steps: 1. Collect from moving sources 2. Boil 1 minute (3 at altitude) 3. Use purification tablets 4. Or use portable filters 5. Store in clean containers 6. Avoid stagnant water
🔥
Fire Starting
Build and maintain safe fires
Steps: 1. Gather tinder, kindling, fuel 2. Build fire ring with stones 3. Use matches or lighter 4. Start with tinder, add kindling 5. Never leave unattended 6. Fully extinguish before leaving
🗺️
Navigation
Read maps and use compass
Steps: 1. Orient map with compass 2. Use landmarks for reference 3. Mark planned route 4. Check progress regularly 5. If lost, stay put 6. Signal for help with whistle
🍖
Food Procurement
Find and prepare safe food
Steps: 1. Know edible local plants 2. NEVER eat unknown plants 3. Fish with proper technique 4. Follow local regulations 5. Cook thoroughly 6. Store safely from animals
⛺
Camp Hygiene
Maintain sanitary conditions
Steps: 1. Latrine 100+ feet from water 2. Wash hands before eating 3. Dishes away from water source 4. Hang food in bear bag 5. Burn paper, pack out trash 6. Keep camp clean
🧗
Rope & Knots
Essential knots for survival
Key knots: 1. Bowline - fixed loop 2. Clove hitch - attach to trees 3. Taut-line - adjust tension 4. Square knot - join ropes 5. Practice tying blind 6. Know 4-5 essential knots
🧭
Wilderness First Aid
Remote emergency treatment
Steps: 1. Access condition stability 2. Move to safe location 3. Control bleeding 4. Treat for shock 5. Immobilize injuries 6. Plan evacuation or wait for help
🌙
Night Survival
Stay safe after dark
Steps: 1. Create adequate shelter early 2. Gather firewood before dark 3. Stay warm with blanket/layers 4. Keep light source ready 5. Do NOT travel after dark 6. Use stars/moon for navigation
📱
Signaling & Communication
Signal for rescue
Steps: 1. Use whistle (three blasts = SOS) 2. Bright colors visible from air 3. Mirror flashing if available 4. Create large ground signals 5. Stay put when lost 6. Preserve phone batteries
🎒
10 Essentials
Carry these on every hike
Items: 1. Map & compass 2. Flashlight 3. First aid kit 4. Matches/lighter 5. Knife 6. Emergency shelter 7. Water bottle 8. Food 9. Extra clothes 10. Whistle

🌿 Florida Field Guide

Identify 150+ Central Florida wildlife, plants, and tracks

🦌
White-tailed Deer
Most common large mammal. Cloven hoof tracks (heart-shaped). White flag tail when fleeing. Dawn/dusk near forest edges.
🦝
Raccoon
Extremely common near campgrounds. Dexterous paws. Ringed tail, masked face. Will raid food storage. Bear bag all food.
🐻
Florida Black Bear
Rare but present. 200-300 lbs. Shaggy black fur, rounded ears. Found in swamps and forests. Never approach.
🦊
Grey Fox
Small canine, 3-4 lbs. Only fox that climbs trees. Salt-and-pepper colored. Nocturnal, rarely seen.
🦨
Striped Skunk
Black with white stripe. 2-4 lbs. Known for spray defense. Nocturnal. Avoid if approached.
🐿️
Fox Squirrel
Large squirrel, orange-red fur. Bushy tail. Common in oak forests. Diurnal, very active at dawn.
🦫
Beaver
Large rodent, 30-60 lbs. Flat tail, orange teeth. Build dams in freshwater. Nocturnal.
🦌
Fallow Deer
Non-native, similar to white-tailed. Spotted coat. Found in some central Florida areas.
🐭
Marsh Rabbit
Small, reddish-brown. Lives in wetlands. Swims well. Rarely ventures far from water.
🦝
Coyote
Medium canine, 30-40 lbs. Tan/grey fur. Expanding range in Florida. Nocturnal, hear howling at night.
🐊
American Alligator
6-14 ft long. Log-like shape near water. Eyes glow red in flashlight. Found in all freshwater.
🐢
Gopher Tortoise
Protected species. Domed shell, stumpy legs. Creates 10 ft deep burrows. Home to 350+ animal species.
🐍
Eastern Diamondback Rattlesnake
Largest venomous snake. Diamond pattern, triangular head. Loud rattle. Can reach 8 feet. Give wide berth.
🦎
Green Anole
Florida's native anole. Can change green to brown. Males show pink dewlap. Excellent insect controllers.
🐢
Florida Softshell Turtle
Flat, leathery shell. Long neck, small head. Fast swimmers. Found in rivers and lakes.
🐍
Water Moccasin (Cottonmouth)
Venomous, aquatic snake. Dark, heavy body. Found near water. White mouth when threatened.
🦗
Florida Scrub Lizard
Small, spiny. Only in Florida scrub habitat. Blue coloring on males. Extremely agile.
🐢
Box Turtle
Domed shell, hinged plastron. Brown coloring. Can close shell completely. Shy and slow-moving.
🐍
Corn Snake
Non-venomous, beautiful orange/red. Found in various habitats. Docile and beneficial.
🦎
Leopard Gecko
Spotted pattern, yellowish. Nocturnal. Non-native but established in some areas.
🦅
Bald Eagle
Year-round resident. White head/tail only at age 4-5. 8 ft wingspan. Near lakes and coastlines.
🦩
Roseate Spoonbill
Unmistakable pink color. Spatula-shaped bill. Shallow water sweeper. Healthy wetland indicator.
🐦
Sandhill Crane
Tall grey birds, red forehead patch. Walk in pairs or families. Loud bugling call. Mate for life.
🦅
Red-shouldered Hawk
Medium raptor. Rufous shoulders, barred wings. Common in woodlands. Loud kee-ahh call.
🐝
Florida Scrub Jay
Bright blue and grey. Only in Florida scrub. Threatened species. Extremely curious, may land on you.
🦉
Barred Owl
Large, stocky owl. Rounded head, no ear tufts. Deep hooting call. Nocturnal hunter.
🦜
Pileated Woodpecker
Large black woodpecker, red crest. Loud drumming. Excavates large rectangular holes.
🦅
Osprey
Fish-hunting raptor. White underside, dark back. Hovers over water then dives. Builds large stick nests.
🦢
White Ibis
White plumage, long curved bill. Wades in shallow water. Colonial nesters. Seen in flocks.
🦆
Wood Duck
Colorful male, iridescent green. Perches in trees. Cavity nester. Dabbling dabbler, not a diver.
🦋
Monarch Butterfly
Orange/black, white spot border. Major migration corridor. Travel 3,000 miles. Fall migration peak.
🦋
Swallowtail Butterfly
Large, yellow with black stripes. Distinctive tail extensions on wings. Fast, erratic flight.
🪲
Lubber Grasshopper
Florida's largest grasshopper, 3 inches. Yellow/black/red coloring. Flightless. Warning coloration.
🐝
Honeybee
Essential pollinator. Golden/brown. Build hexagonal honeycomb. Colony of 20,000-80,000 bees.
🦂
Florida Scorpion
Small, yellow. Venomous but rarely fatal. Nocturnal. Glow under UV light.
🕷️
Orb Weaver Spider
Large, colorful. Build geometric webs. Beneficial insect controllers. Non-aggressive.
🦗
Cricket
Brown/black, chirping at night. Excellent hearing. Omnivorous. Important food source for wildlife.
🪰
Damselfly
Slender, colored wings. Predatory. Important wetland indicator. Hover over water.
🦟
Mosquito
Small blood-suckers. Only females bite. Disease carriers. Breeding in still water.
🦑
Dragonfly
Large wings, iridescent. Aerial hunters. Eat thousands of mosquitoes daily. Prehistoric lineage.
🌴
Sabal Palm
Florida's state tree. Fan-shaped fronds, bootjack marks. Berries eaten by wildlife. Hurricane-resistant.
🌿
Spanish Moss
Not a moss—epiphytic bromeliad. Hangs in grey-green curtains. Provides animal habitat. Non-parasitic.
🌴
Saw Palmetto
Low-growing fan palm. Razor-sharp serrated stems. Dense clusters in scrub. Wildlife food source.
🌺

🌲

🌳

🌳

🌿

🪢 Knot Library

Tap any knot card to flip and see step-by-step instructions

🪢
Square Knot
Join two ropes of equal thickness
Steps: 1. Cross right rope over left 2. Cross left over right 3. Pull firmly 4. Knot should lie flat and symmetric
🏹
Bowline
Create a fixed loop that won't slip
Steps: 1. Make small loop in rope 2. "Rabbit comes out of hole" (thread through) 3. "Goes around tree" (wrap around standing line) 4. "Back in hole" (thread through loop) 5. Pull tight
🏕️
Clove Hitch
Attach rope to pole or post
Steps: 1. Wrap rope around post 2. Cross rope over itself 3. Wrap around post again 4. Thread through the crossing 5. Pull tight
8️⃣
Figure Eight
Stopper knot at rope ends
Steps: 1. Make a loop 2. Wrap rope underneath 3. Thread through top of loop 4. Pull tight 5. Creates bulky, reliable stopper
⛺
Taut-line Hitch
Adjustable tension on tent lines
Steps: 1. Wrap rope around standing line twice 2. Wrap around once more (outside) 3. Thread through all three coils 4. Slides to adjust, locks when loaded
🔗
Two Half Hitches
Attach to rings, rails, posts
Steps: 1. Make first half hitch around object 2. Thread working end through loop 3. Make second half hitch 4. Both face same direction 5. Pull tight
🎯
Reef Knot
Secure bundle or join thin lines
Steps: 1. Right over left, left under right 2. Left over right, right under left 3. Pull both ends 4. Forms symmetrical knot
🪝
Girth Hitch
Attach rope to rings or loops
Steps: 1. Pass working end through ring 2. Wrap around standing line 3. Pass through ring again 4. Thread under first wrap 5. Pull tight
🌀
Slip Knot
Adjustable loop that tightens
Steps: 1. Make small loop 2. Pass working end through loop 3. Loop the working end around standing line 4. Thread through original loop 5. Tighten to desired size
🔀
Sheet Bend
Join ropes of different thickness
Steps: 1. Make bend in thicker rope 2. Thread thinner rope through bend 3. Wrap around both parts 4. Thread back through bend 5. Pull tight
🎪
Trucker's Hitch
Create mechanical advantage for tightening
Steps: 1. Make a fixed loop (use bowline) 2. Thread rope through anchor point 3. Wrap through loop 4. Secure end with clove hitch 5. Create 3:1 mechanical advantage
🌉
Timber Hitch
Drag logs or drag heavy objects
Steps: 1. Wrap rope around log 2. Cross working end over standing line 3. Wrap around standing line twice 4. Thread through first wrap 5. Pull to tighten
🧗
Double Overhand
Strong stopper knot at rope end
Steps: 1. Make initial loop 2. Wrap around standing line twice 3. Thread working end through loop 4. Pull tight 5. Larger and more secure than single overhand
⚓
Anchor Hitch
Tie rope to anchor ring or carabiner
Steps: 1. Thread rope through anchor ring 2. Make one wrap around standing line 3. Make half hitch 4. Second half hitch 5. Secure and tested
🎨
Marlinspike Hitch
Temporary loop using tool or stick
Steps: 1. Place marlinspike through loop 2. Make half hitch around spike 3. Keep loop over spike 4. Remove spike when done 5. Loop disappears
